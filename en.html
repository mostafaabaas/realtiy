Guide 1 · Walkthroughs

How a unit walkthrough should be shot to sell the unit — not the compound's beauty shots

A unit video that sells is shot as a buyer's walk, not an architecture commercial. Aerial landscaping and lake shots earn admiration for the project — but what converts into a booked visit is an honest tour inside a unit the viewer can picture living in. We build the tour like this:

Walk in the order of a buyer's day

  • Start at the unit's front door. It is the first moment of any real viewing, and the viewer decides from it whether to continue. Never open with an aerial or a project logo.
  • The order is the order of living: entrance, living room, kitchen, bedrooms, bathrooms, then the balcony or terrace. Each room gets one sentence about its actual use — not poetic description.
  • Honest scale. Real furniture or familiar-size objects in frame so the eye measures the space correctly. Wide lenses that inflate rooms produce views — and disappointed visits.
  • The practical things buyers actually ask about: storage, how the kitchen works, how many bathrooms serve whom, what the balcony fits, where laundry goes. These are the questions deals close on.
  • Natural light, real sound. The tour is filmed in daylight with windows open and the creator speaking in their normal voice. Excess gloss shouts "advertising" and puts every word under suspicion.

And finish outside the unit

The last part of the tour is the unit's context: the project around it, the amenities that will genuinely be used, and the location against the city. A buyer is not buying metres — they are buying what their day will look like. But the order is fixed: the unit first, the compound last — the reverse of most project videos, and why most of them don't sell.

Guide 2 · Off-plan launches

UGC for off-plan launches: build trust before there is a building

In off-plan, the product being sold is trust. The buyer pays into a promise — so the content that works is not glossy renders but a sequence of evidence that this developer actually delivers and this project is actually moving. Good launch content is built from five layers:

  • The delivered record first. Creator tours inside the developer's previously delivered projects: real units, real residents, real finishing. The strongest answer to "will they deliver as promised?".
  • Construction progress, dated and repeated. The same filming angles on site every period, with the date visible. A regular progress series builds a credibility no single launch video can buy.
  • The show unit, honestly. Filmed as a show unit, with a clear statement of what is included in delivery and what is display only. Ambiguity here becomes complaints later.
  • Concern-question videos. When is delivery, really? What does the finishing look like? How do maintenance and services work? Short videos, one question at a time, from people who visited the site themselves.
  • The published payment plan — and only that. The creator explains the official announced plan exactly as published. No return promises, no "prices will rise", no investment talk. Content that crosses this line destroys the trust the entire launch stands on.

The render rule

Renders and designs have their place — explaining the concept and the design. But any render appearing in content is clearly labelled a design, not footage. Confusing the two is the fastest way to destroy a launch's credibility, and a buyer who discovers the confusion themselves does not come back.

Guide 3 · The brief

What to put in a creator brief for a real-estate tour

A real-estate tour brief is not an ordinary product brief — the creator here speaks about sensitive financial and legal facts, so the brief must draw the line precisely between what is said and what is never said. Its structure is five parts:

1 · Location facts

The district and area, the nearest landmarks and services, and distances and times exactly as the developer publishes them — the creator rounds no number and invents no location advantage that is not in the project's official materials.

2 · Unit facts

Area, layout (rooms and bathrooms), floor and type, and the delivered finishing level — and if the tour is in a furnished show unit, the brief states plainly what in frame is included in delivery and what is not.

3 · Payment-plan facts: may say / may not say

May say
  • The official published payment plan: down payment, instalments and their term
  • The officially announced delivery date
  • Live official launch offers, with their dates
May not say
  • Any promise of investment return or guaranteed rent
  • Expectations of price appreciation
  • Self-invented "approximate" delivery dates, or unit prices not approved in writing by the developer

4 · Disclosure and filming rules

Paid-partnership disclosure appears in the content itself per platform rules. Renders are named renders. Access permits and drone filming — where used — are production's responsibility and arranged before the day; a creator never films in a place or a way that was not permitted.

5 · Must-show moments and format

A full tour in natural walking order, vertical format, the creator on camera or in voiceover per their account's format, and context shots last. Deliverables and usage scope are agreed in writing before filming: the video count, whether raw footage is included, and usage — organic and paid, and for how long.

Guide 4 · Saudi market

Content marketing for Saudi real-estate projects: Riyadh, Jeddah and the Eastern Province

The Saudi buyer meets your project on platforms before visiting your sales gallery — so project content is not a side ad; it is the first sales tour. And every city has a different audience: Riyadh looks for new districts and growth, Jeddah for the view and the lifestyle, the Eastern Province for proximity to work centres and family calm. Winning content speaks each city's language and builds only on the project's official facts.

1 · Start with the real tour

A properly briefed creator tour of a show unit or an actual site answers the buyer's first question: "what does the place really look like?" — a natural walking order, real light, and the district's context at the end of the tour. Renders are named renders, and a show unit is declared a show unit.

2 · Payment talk only from the project's official materials

Payment plans, delivery dates and launch offers are stated exactly as the developer publishes them — with no promise of investment return or price appreciation, in Saudi Arabia as in every market we work in. This is not just legal caution; it is what keeps the audience's trust in the project and the developer.

3 · A content rhythm across the project's life

An off-plan launch needs trust content (the developer, the location, the masterplan), the selling phase needs tours and unit detail, and the run-up to handover needs life content: services, schools and the daily feel of living there. One connected series beats one big ad that gets forgotten.

FAQ

What developers and brokers ask before a first pack

Short answers, in writing — the same limits that end up in the brief and the agreement.

Can creators film inside our compound or project?

Filming is arranged in writing before anything else. The developer grants access to the agreed units and areas, and we coordinate the tour plan, creators and schedule — so no filming happens without a clear permit, and no creator enters a place that was not agreed.

What can a creator say about payment plans and prices?

A creator states only the published facts in the brief: the official payment plan, the announced delivery date, and the unit's specifications. Strictly forbidden: promises of returns or price appreciation, invented delivery dates, or figures not approved by the developer — all written into the brief itself before filming.

Our project is still under construction (off-plan) — what would we film?

We film what builds trust before delivery: the developer's actually delivered projects, show units and real finishing, dated construction progress, and tours of the actual site. Renders are labelled as renders whenever they appear — no design is ever sold as reality.

Can we run the videos in our ads? What are the usage rights?

Usage scope is agreed in writing before production: where the videos may run (project pages, your organic channels, your paid ad accounts), on which platforms, and for how long. The scope you buy is the scope in your agreement — and whitelisting through the creator's account is arranged per creator when your pack includes it.

How long does a creator tour batch take from agreement to delivery?

The exact schedule is approved in a written plan before production. It depends on the number of units and creators, access arrangements and creator availability — so the plan is built around your sales calendar and launch timing, not a generic promise.

Are prices published?

No prices are published. Packs have a defined structure — a number of tours, videos and a usage scope — and are priced in a written quote after a free consultation or brief, based on the project's size and the content it needs.
